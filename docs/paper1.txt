Paper : 
Distilling the Knowledge in a Neural Network

Link : 
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1503.02531

Main points :
1. Soft targets contain more information than hard labels.
2. A large teacher/ensemble can transfer its learned behavior to a smaller 
student through those soft targets.
3. Temperature is what makes the hidden class relationships in the teacher's 
predictions visible to the student.

Summary :

Hinton et al. (2015) introduced knowledge distillation as a framework for transferring 
knowledge from a high-capacity teacher model to a more compact student model. 
The approach trains the student using the teacher’s soft output distributions, 
which encode inter-class relationships and information about the teacher’s predictive 
uncertainty that is not captured by the hard labels alone. Also a temperature parameter is used to 
smoothen the output distribution and expose this additional information during the training phase.
The authors shows that distilled models can approach the performance of larger models and 
ensembles while substantially reducing inference-time computational costs. 
The study further demonstrates the applicability of distillation to speech recognition 
and large-scale classification through the use of specific specialized student models.
